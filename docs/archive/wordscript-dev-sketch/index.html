    Where your words go.

    Every dictation tool puts text at the cursor. That is the first of three, and it is the one that is becoming a commodity.

    WordScript

    The cursor. Hold the hotkey and speak, and the text lands in whatever window has focus. The mode decides which text lands, and the mode is the only thing the transform stage reads.

    What it changed

    Context. A dictation, a meeting, an uploaded file and a pasted link are the same kind of record. What accumulates is one living object rather than a folder of loose transcripts, and it sits in a directory you own.

    On your disk

    Plain files, in a directory you named. Your editor, your grep and your agent already know how to open them.

    The agent. One orchestrator drives your coding agents, and WordScript talks to nobody else. When it reaches a decision it cannot make, it asks you out loud and waits. You can start the work by speaking, too.

    It counts what it did for you.

    That speaking beats typing is not our finding, and for most of the time computers could listen at all it was not even true. The three measurements below are other people's. What is ours is the second half: the app counts what it actually did for you, and reads those figures out of a file on your own disk.

    1. 1999 Speaking lost.
      spoken 13.6wpm
      typed 32.5wpm

      Transcription by voice against keyboard and mouse, 24 users, three commercial recognisers. Karat, Halverson, Horn and Karat, Patterns of Entry and Correction in Large Vocabulary Continuous Speech Recognition Systems, CHI 1999. Figures as reported in Ruan et al. 2016.

    2. 2016 It turned over.
      spoken 161.2wpm
      typed 53.5wpm

      English short messages, 32 participants, with 20.4 percent fewer errors than the keyboard. Measured on a phone. Ruan, Wobbrock, Liou, Ng and Landay, Comparing Speech and Keyboard Text Entry for Short Messages in Two Languages on Touchscreen Phones, 2016.

    3. 2018 The desk, checked.
      typed, physical keyboard 51.6wpm

      Mean 51.56, SD 20.2, across 168,000 participants. Dhakal, Feit, Kristensson and Oulasvirta, Observations on Typing from 136 Million Keystrokes, CHI 2018.

    The 2016 study ran on a phone, and that is the objection worth answering rather than hiding: its touchscreen keyboard came out at 53.5 wpm, and a physical keyboard measured across 168,000 people comes out at 51.6. The typing side barely moves. What moved is the other side, and what held it back in 1999 was correction, not speed. That is the half the modes do.

    The dictation half, done properly.

    The base layer still has to be worth using every day. This is what it does.

    • Profiles

      A profile that actually changes the output.

      Vocabulary, style, delivery mode and model belong to the context you are working in. Writing a commit message and writing to a client are not the same job.

    • Models

      Local or cloud, per job.

      The local runtime as a real option rather than an expert configuration exercise. Chosen per profile, not once for the whole app.

    • The record

      It shows what it changed.

      Raw and delivered are stored apart. Every rule that fired is named, and audio it could not hear is marked as a gap instead of guessed at.

    • Credentials

      Your keys, your machine.

      API keys live in the operating system secret store, and the config file is scrubbed on save. Nothing goes anywhere you did not pick.

    • Sources

      Bring audio in.

      An uploaded file, or a pasted video, podcast or direct audio link, becomes the same kind of record as something you said out loud.

    • Licence

      AGPL-3.0, all of it.

      The whole app, not a core with a paid remainder. Readable before you decide anything.

    The honest answers.

    Can I download it yet?

    No. There is no release and no date. It runs from source on macOS, Windows and Linux today, and the fastest way to follow the work is Discord or the repository.

    Is this another wrapper around Whisper?

    The recogniser is swappable and Whisper is one of the options. Speech to text is becoming a commodity, so the recogniser is not the product. What is: what accumulates from what you said, and what can act on it.

    Does my audio leave the machine?

    Only when you pick a cloud model for that profile. The local lane runs on your own hardware and reaches nothing. The choice is per profile, and it is visible.

    How is this different from the other open dictation apps?

    They are good, and the dictation half genuinely overlaps. The difference is where what you said ends up. Their context lives inside their product. WordScript writes it into a directory your own tools can already open, and does not maintain a second integration surface to get it back out.

    When will it be released?

    There is no date. The gates that are still open are recorded in the repository, and that list is the only honest answer to this question.

    Can I help build it?

    Yes, and that is the point of this page. The repository is open and the room where the work gets argued out is Discord.
